# Draft 2: 2 January 2026

I've been served a lot of content lately about the way that we as a society consume media. 
I've also been picking up more comments that occur within other videos about the nature of content creation. 
I do agree with the idea that, by and large, we as a society are too used to being stimulated. 
Much as I enjoy listening to things at 3x speed, there is a real argument to be made against it, especially if I'm doing something else at the same time.

I now turn off video game music the moment I start it. 
That means whatever work a composer put into their craft is now wasted.^[of course, given how many people refuse to pay musicians or artists, I would not be surprised to find out that the "music" in many of my games is not composed for the source, if it's music at all (AI can't make music)]

I've been wanting to read the stack of library books I checked out for all of my time back in Iowa. 
I've read none of them.^[though, to be fair, I've read about 3200 pages of a web novel that I wanted to catch up on]

So, as the new year breaks and the world is soon to celebrate another Dr. Rebelsky, let's consider what I consume.

1. On YouTube, I currently subscribe to 149 channels. A future blog post will have to consider which of these I want to stay subscribed to.^[added to the [[todo.html to do list]]]  
2. I am signed up for a bunch of web serials (22 by my count), many of which I neither follow nor intend to follow again in any realistic time frame.^[see above]  
The ones that still get updated should be removed, and the ones that I wouldn't come back to if they started posting again should as well. 
3. I scroll, though less than I used to! That's progress  
4. Music; I by and large put my phone on discover mode, which I'm willing to believe is not what I should do  
5. Music; I by and large listen to music while doing other things. That's not inherently wrong, I don't think. However, there is a real part of me that's fundamentally uncomfortable with the idea of music being reduced to background noise. That's something to think about for sure, but also maybe the answer is in just picking an album and listening all the way through it on loop until I get bored of it? Or take it one step further: pick an album, listen to one song on loop, and only then shift to the next song. That feels appropriately insane, I think. 

I think that those five things are all good enough things to think about in consideration of the new year. 
Let's go ahead and trust ourselves to do that.

I don't think that I actually said anything about consumption. 
Eh. 
I trust my readers.

# Draft 1: 2 January 2026

(title of piece during this draft "Thinking about Music (and other things)"

Let's think about music for a little bit!  
I was listening to Polyphonic's end of year reflection^[which I watched on Nebula, so won't be linking here], and he mentioned that he's been listening to a lot of digital DJ sets lately. 
As he said it, I felt myself cringe a little bit. 
Looking back, that's probably moreso because I think of DJ as a weird term than anything else. 
A few seconds later, however, he explained his justification.^[I'm going to quote, even though I don't remember it exactly and won't be looking it up]

"I want to listen to music curated by people, not algorithms"

I thought that was a really nice idea. 
Also, I do really appreciate a lot of his content; by and large he does in-depth reviews of music he loves. 
I also enjoy Todd in the Shadows^[also on Nebula], though his takes are more often in-depth reviews of music he hates. 
Of course, I cannot talk about music Youtubers^[which is my term for all video creators who make YT like content, regardless of where they post it or I consume it] without talking about 12Tone. 
He recently talked about vertical and horizontal harmony, which is something that I absolutely adore. 
For that reason, if nothing else, I'm going to keep consuming his content.

Of course, there's a major issue between the way that I tend to consume YT content and these channels. 
As some of my friends have complained, I tend not to consume media at single speeds. 
I've got any number of justifications for this, which all tend to boil down to me generally caring more about being able to talk to friends about media that they enjoy than any actual appreciation of media itself. 
And, as someone who listens to and reads a lot of progression fantasy, I'm becoming more and more convinced that many of the big-name studios in the space don't record their audio with the intent of being listened to at single speed.

The issue comes, however, in the fact that music is one of the few forms of audible art that I feel like I do actually fundamentally experience it differently at different speeds. 
Even when working with MuseScore's auto-playback, changing the tempo fundamentally changes my experience of the piece. 
Writing it out, that feels kind of obvious; music is often defined as sound in time, if I change the time, I'm obviously changing the music. 
These channels, which are music-focused, then, probably aren't the best for listening to quickly.

They also use generally decent animations, if I recall correctly, and I do tend to do other activities while listening to YT content. 
That's it's own bucket of worms for me to think about, but.

Hmmm.

I guess this blog is about consumption, rather than music. 
Time to change the title and try again.
