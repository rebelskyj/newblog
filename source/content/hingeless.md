# Free-Flowing Thoughts

I did some journaling by hand yesterday, and I think that it might be good for me to have it in a slightly less ephemeral form than a random piece of paper I'll lose. With this in mind, I'm going to have below this the unedited (as far as feels plausible) reflection. Authors notes will occur somewhere ? Unsure where

## Setting the scene.

I was thinking about how, of the many things I do, journaling is probably the best use of my time. At that point, I finally confronted the fact that I hate capital-based descriptions of passing time. I looked through different options before finally setting on "bind", because fortify and grow have too much of an inherent changing character to them. And now to the reflection.

The astute might notice a distinct lack of line breaks in the following lines. It's among the reasons my hand-written journals have caused friends concern in the past

N.B. my common short hand (w/ instead of with, \& instead of and, etc) will be written in long hand here

How does X bind me to eudaimonia? Bind to good spirit and how?

What does X bind me to? Given yesterday's post about connection, may be good now. What is my time bound to? Sure. To what do I fasten and bind time. In : re philosophy, I even struggle with phenom. I think. Can one have a physic without a metaphysic? I want to explore a metaphysic of binding. From the smallest of particles to the largest of galactic filament, we are a universe of fundamental binding. As a chemist, I have oft described ~~life~~ molecules as bind. Do I believe in consciousness as irreducible? I don't know. I must explore this for further before I can make any real claims. At a human level, I do not think I can break to not doubting that I think. The first thing I can draw on is that I impact the world around me. Do I exist outside of the bonds I form with others? When I intentionally sever a bond of friendship, the bond still remains. Infinite small threads of who I am, come from and go to others. My semi-joking comment of no electrons kinda works here; ceases to be discrete and instead becomes naught but connection. I do not know if I can think of the world primarily as things. To be bound is to exist. A shirt is a thread that itself binds us to one another. Someone chose these color, sewed the garment, drilled the oil that became the plastic. I bind myself in ways I can never know; a life saved by my action, even if I do not know it, and they do not know it either.When I bind myself to the inanimate, I bind myself away from humanity, I become less human. When I bind my time (am I just time?) to journals, I find myself seeing more bonds and tending to them better. So if I am to be the ~~truest~~ most human I can be, I must be fully bound to humanity. I am bound to all other people. I am bound to the earth and air. I am bound to the sound that I have sent out. When, for a moment, the choir's members all forget themselves, the bindings that pull them to the dead past and unknowable future fade away. They are bound to one another, to the sound, the light, the sight. What I find is an issue of modern life is that we want to be self-bound. This is nonsense. You cannot bind to yourself. All you can do is fail to bind to other, to let the binding you can see slowly fade. What is light but a true form of binding? Until a photon strikes, it is essentially non-existent. When we first see some new star, the bound universe grows. Knowledge only exists when bound. H 12 cm line only works if (cm), H, H0 not H+, rest frame, excitation. Molecules only absorb and emit via interaction. Because everything takes time, what is a photon but a thread through time? When I see, truly, I am bound by light, by energy which reaches out eternal forward, eternal back. When the final black hole gives off its final light, the final bond will die, and creation with it too. At that first moment of creation, the Big Bang, all was bound to single point. When it released, in absence of Binding, energy expands. Entropy is the death of all things. Entropy is the bond that only breaks. But, without breaking some bonds, no other bonds can form. Monastic, because cannot see the binding when the other is there. Only when feeling another outside the obvious bindings of physical matter do we see how we are one. The non-physical bonds are the most interesting to me. The flash of light of knowledge revealed, the music which springs from places unknown. We bind them to the broader world; I have calculus of limiting rates because Leibniz and Newton ~~took the~~ spun the ^[unknown word here (?? icarer? scar maybe?)] thread of knowledge from the light of truth. Logic itself cannot reveal the true song. Hmm is binding the metaphor I want or is it song? I think like all things, words alone must fail. The binding is the song, the universe around us. We cannot become as gods because gods are by their nature static. Only living will can bind and be. To become as a god is to Die in the deepest sense. To not see bonds even by their braking, to not exist at all.

Time in prayer is at a minimum a place to create (find? see?) new bindings to one another. At best, a communion to Divinity, Truth, and Song.

So... that sure was something. There are absolutely parts and pieces of this that I think can be expanded. However, even just typing out the page had me reliving many of the same thoughts that went through me as I wrote it the first time. That's shockingly exhausting, finding the bottom of the well not through creative expression to some large degree but through simple meditation on a single topic, writing only by hand.

If I had lined or dotted paper, it would have been much easier to read. I remember in high school forensics class we learned a downwards slope to someone's writing means they feel negative. I'm not sure if it was how I was holding the clipboard or if I was actually feeling down, but I was a solid 5-6 line sloped down by the end of the first page.

Looking forward, I do want to spend some more time exploring this idea of metaphysics of binding. I know that there has to be someone else out there who has, even if only as a thought experiment, written something about this. Apparently there's a group that won an award for a way to solve the problem of evil in a traditionally Christian apologetic; creation was set under the purview of angels who either neglected their duties or actively sought to create the world where suffering is a requisite part of evolution. I do find that kind of fun as an idea.
