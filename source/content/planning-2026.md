## Draft 1: 2025 28 December

I don't entirely know how this is going to work out, but I'm hopeful that I'll be able to find a way to convert this md file into the HTML. Given that the extant system has an intermediate conversion to md, I hope it will work?^[Woo a footnote, even if the nested ones aren't super happy]

Anyways, let's talk about our good friend the upcoming year.
I can look at reflections on other years, and plans for past upcoming years.
But, I don't really want to do that.

I know that if I look at my plan for 2024, I will notice the huge absence of "learn to live with my mother's death".
If I look at my plan for 2025, I'm sure that I will have something about learning to live.
Neither of those is what I want to focus on, though.

I want to think about the me I want to see this time in 2026, and then start back-tracking to see what I would do to get there.
What is the best possible version of me a year from now?

In a perfect world, I would be completely at peace with myself, the broader universe, and everything.
In an optimal version of today's world, I think that I would feel that I am doing my part to combat systemic injustice and societal ill.
In a realistic version of today's world, I'd just like to be better.

What does it mean to be better?

What are things that I want to do?

I want to find a new place to live.
Much as I've loved living in the same place for years^[and only partially because I like not moving into a new location. I've liked my landlady and the maintenance people. I generally enjoy that I'm close to downtown when I want to be there], I do want to live outside of where the frats are at this point in my life.
It would be great to have a shorter commute.

I want to do more in analog form.
That's a big goal, and I'm honestly not totally sure what all it looks like.
I think that part of it is reading library books, rather than solely e-books and web serials.
What web serials I decide to read, I can also presumably print out.
There's a question for sure about where to print things, but that's something I can ask myself later.

I think that there's something to be said for buying a printer^[though then we get into the question of laser versus inkjet versus color laser versus I'm sure there's a black and white ink option], because I don't want to have a "why are you printing so many pages" conversation at work, even if I think that it's unlikely.
Other than that, though, there's the whole question of whether it's better to spend time and effort bookbinding or just do the quick and dirty bookbinding I've been doing at work lately.

In a perfect world, I would love to have a way to staple halfway down a page of paper.
It feels intentional that the average stapler is half an inch too short to staple down the middle of the page.

Anyways, read more, especially analog.

As those I've been texting these past few days might note, I want to be into letter writing more.
I like writing letters, and I think that there's inherent value in writing letters by hand and sending them in the USPS.
What the value is, I'm not totally sure.
Also given my whole rant about intrinsic and extrinsic, I suppose there's what's closest to intrinsic as I really get for motivation.
I just know that I want to start hanging all the letters I get, because I love knowing that others think of me.
I think that some people bind the letters they receive?

I want to actually and actively visit friends.
Now that I've finished with graduate school, and many of my friends have as well, I worry that I'll never see them again if I don't make the effort to.

I want to blog here more?

I think?

I think that it's good for me to write.
I think that it's good for me to also type.
I think that it's good for me to have writing that I don't share.^[as a recent video I watched points out, there's a danger to not feeling like you're ever in private]  
I think that it's good for me to have writing that I can reference wherever I am or that I can send people when I want.

I want to do more music.
Partially, I want to do more semi-classical composition.
I have a VST now that makes choral music doable.
Partially I want to do more singer-songwriter writing, because I like the idea of being able to do that.
I want to do some recording, though that may be a "when I have a place that I can set up with noise cancelling" kind of thing.
I also want to get back into open mics.
Now that my friends who I played with have graduated, I have to do that alone or find others.

I want to explore my Jewish identity.
I've loved the two times I've been at Chabad, and I think that more time there or other places would do me good.
I know that I have one prior friend and at least one or two new friends at Chabad, so that has a strong pull.
However, I think that I have other friends at other shuls/synagogues in the area, so I should also check in there.

I want to learn Hebrew.
It's an essential part of being Jewish, at least from literally every Jewish event I've been to.
It's also just a beautiful language, and a key part of connecting to the history of Judaism.
I've learned enough Latin before that I can translate if I need.
It would be cool to at least be able to read the script during prayer, even if not all the words are totally meaningful.

I want to learn Quebecois.
I think it's beautiful, and I feel weird that we accept that Mexican Spanish is valid but not French Canadian.

I'm going to call this draft here and hopefully have more to say in the next one.
